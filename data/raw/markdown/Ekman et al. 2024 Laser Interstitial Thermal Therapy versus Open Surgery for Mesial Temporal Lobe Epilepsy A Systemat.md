# INTRODUCTION

Epilepsy is a widespread neurological disorder, affecting up to 0.6% of the global population. 1 Temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) often coexists with neurocognitive impairments such as verbal and memory deficits, further compromising the quality of life. 2,3 Mesial temporal lobe epilepsy (mTLE), the most prevalent form of drug-resistant epilepsy (DRE), usually challenges patients by resisting optimal antiseizure medication regimes and causing frequent, uncontrolled seizures that severely affect daily life. While temporal lobe resection Epilepsy surgery offers a vital treatment option for drug-resistant mesial temporal lobe epilepsy, with temporal lobe resection (TLR) and magnetic resonanceguided laser interstitial thermal therapy (MRgLITT) being fundamental interventions. This meta-analysis specifically examines seizure outcomes at extended follow-up periods exceeding 24 months, visual field deficits as measured by perimetry, and complication rates both overall and categorized based on duration as minor (transient <6 months) or major (persistent >6 months) to inform clinical decision-making. For seizure freedom, TLR was superior, with 72.5% [65.6%, 78.5%] of patients achieving postoperative seizure freedom compared to 57.1% [51.2%, 62.7%] for MRgLITT (P value <0.01). Visual field deficits were observed in 79.4% [59.5%, 91.0%] of TLR patients and 49.8% [23.6%, 76.0%] of MRgLITT patients, a difference not reaching statistical significance (P value: 0.08). Overall complication rates were 11.4% [7.4%, 17.2%] for TLR and 6.5% [3.3%, 12.3%] for MRgLITT (P value 0.15). Major complications occurred in 2.0% [1.1%, 3.09%] of TLR cases and 2.7% [1.4%, 5.2%] of MRgLITT cases (P value 0.54), while minor complications were significantly more frequent with TLR at 9.9% [6.4%, 15.0%] versus MRgLITT's 4.1% [1.9%, 8.4%] (P value 0.04). MRgLITT had a more favorable outcome regarding confrontation naming, while more studies are needed regarding verbal memory to be able to draw firm conclusions. TLR provides superior seizure freedom but comes with an increased risk of transient complications. Although there was no statistical significance in visual field deficits, the trend suggests a higher frequency with TLR. The study's extensive data analysis, including rigorous sensitivity checks, ensures the robustness of these conclusions, reflecting a comprehensive analysis of the available data at this time point.

However, direct comparisons between these 2 methods remain scarce. This gap underscores the value of a metaanalysis of observational studies for each method, providing a comprehensive comparative assessment of their effectiveness and safety in treating mTLE.

Surgical intervention for TLE has a long history; the first series of temporal lobectomies for epilepsy was reported in 1950 by Penfield and Flanigan. 6 The procedure of anterior temporal lobectomy (ATL) has been further developed over the years and is now usually performed with a small lateral resection sparing the superior temporal gyrus and maximizing medial resection. 7 Selective amygdalohippocampectomy (sAHE) was introduced by Niemeyer through a transcortical approach, 8 and later Yasargil introduced the transsylvian sAHE. 9 A more recent development in surgery is MRgLITT, which was first applied for DRE in 2014. 10 MRgLITT is a minimally invasive procedure where a 980 nm diode laser is inserted through a 3.2 mm burr hole, whereafter the brain tissue around the laser is heated, resulting in a thermal injury. 11 The procedure is monitored in near real-time using magnetic resonance thermography, allowing for a controlled thermal injury and the creation of temperature markers to protect brain tissue that should not be heated. For the purposes of this meta-analysis, we have grouped these traditional resective surgeries-ATL and sAHE-under the term 'temporal lobe resection (TLR)' to facilitate comparison with the newer MRgLITT technique.

Each of these surgical approaches comes with its own set of ef ficacy and side-effect pro files. However, there is a scarcity of direct comparative studies between these interventions, making clinical decision-making challenging. In such cases, meta-analyses take on extra significance as they can pool data from different studies to provide a more robust evaluation of treatment ef ficacy and safety. A recent meta-analysis by Kohlhase et al. has suggested that ATL may be more effective in achieving Engel-I outcomes compared to MRgLITT but tends to have a higher, albeit not statistically signi ficant, rate of overall and major complications. 12 As for cognitive outcomes, studies suggest that MRgLITT has an advantage in preserving naming ability over TLR, 13 , 14 although the available evidence regarding verbal learning and memory is insuf ficient to draw de finitive conclusions.

While previous analyses have offered valuable insights, they frequently concentrate on shorter follow-up periods when assessing seizure outcomes. A more comprehensive understanding requires analyzing seizure outcomes over longer follow-up periods. Additionally, it is central to differentiate between complications and expected adverse effects. Accurate evaluation of visual field de ficits is also essential to map how different intervention affects the optic radiation or optic tract.

This study aims to provide a comprehensive, up-to-date comparison of seizure outcomes, complications, visual field deficits, and cognitive outcome after MRgLITT or TLR, thereby aiding the clinical decision-making process in epilepsy surgery.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

The study was performed according to the recommendations outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions 15 together with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis guidelines statement 16 and registered in PROSPERO International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (registration no. CRD42023400355).

## Search Strategy

The search criteria were de fined based on a PICOS framework (Participants, Interventions, Comparators, Outcomes, and Study design) to identify search terms. How do patients with drug-resistant mTLE (participants) who are treated with MRgLITT (intervention) compared with those treated with TLR (comparators) differ concerning seizure freedom, postoperative complications, visual field de ficits, and cognitive outcome? Study design included only original research.

Three databases (PubMed, EMBASE, and the Cochrane library) were extensively searched for publications concerning MRgLITT or TLR for the treatment of temporal lobe epilepsy and outcomes related to seizure freedom, postoperative complications, visual field de ficits, and cognition. The search was conducted on February 27th, 2023, by a single researcher (F.E.) ( Figure 1 ).

The search terms can be found in Supplement A .

## Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

## Inclusion Criteria

1. Type of Studies and Interventions: Prospective or retrospective, peerreviewed cohort studies in English reporting the ef ficacy of MRgLITT, ATL, or sAHE in patients with drugresistant mTLE.

2. Outcomes: Studies reporting on any of the following outcomes.

 Seizure Outcomes: Evaluated using Engel 17 or International League Against Epilepsy 18 scales, with a follow-up time of at least 24 months.

 Visual Field De ficits (VFDs): assessed with perimetry.

 Complication Frequency: Classi fied as transient or permanent or with details on the duration of complications, enabling classi fication into minor (resolving within 6 months) or major (persisting beyond 6 months) categories.

 Cognitive Outcome: verbal memory assessed by WMS-III LM-I or LM-II, 19 verbal learning and memory assessed by California verbal learning test/ Reyauditory verbal learning test 20 , 21 or confrontatio nal naming assessed by Boston Naming Test, 22 regardless of followup time.

3. Sample Size: Studies with a sample size of no fewer than 10 patients.

## Exclusion Criteria

- 1. Type of Studies: Exclude noncohort studies, including randomized controlled trials, controlled trials, predictive studies, and conference abstracts.
- 2. Diagnosis: Exclude studies focusing on conditions other than drug-resistant mTLE.

- 3. Speci fic Patient Groups: Exclude studies exclusively analyzing speci fic patient subgroups, such as those with mesial temporal lobe sclerosis.
- 4. Repeated Publications: Exclude studies that are repeated publications of the same patient cohort analyzing the same outcome.

5. Publication Date: Exclude studies published before the year 2000.

## Justi fications

 The 24-month follow-up period for seizure outcome was selected to strike a balance between capturing longer-term outcomes and ensuring an adequate number of available studies to effectively answer the research question.

 The sample size cut-off of 10 is employed to ensure that included studies are suf ficiently large to potentially impact the meta-analysis.

 Randomized controlled trials (RCTs), controlled trials, and predictive studies were excluded due to fundamental differences in study design and patient selection compared to cohort studies. Including these types of studies would introduce methodological inconsistencies, making it dif ficult to pool and interpret the combined data accurately. Therefore, the review focuses solely on cohort studies for a more cohesive analysis.

## Selection of Studies and Data Extraction

Study Selection. The first author reviewed all titles and abstracts, and any inconsistencies or uncertainties regarding study eligibility were discussed with the senior author (D.N.). Studies that could not be definitively excluded based on title and abstract were subjected to full-text screening. The same researcher then reviewed all remaining full-text articles to determine their suitability for inclusion, with any uncertainties again discussed with the senior author (D.N.).

Data Extraction. Data were systematically extracted and included the following information: authorship, year of publication; study sample characteristics; and relevant participant data based on the PICO framework (i.e., seizure outcome, complication rate, visual field de ficits, and cognitive outcome). Additionally, the type of intervention used in each study was recorded. All data were compiled and managed in a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet.

## Endpoints

The primary endpoints of the study included several key metrics: seizure freedom, complication rate, rate of VFDs. For seizure outcomes, the proportion of patients achieving Engel I or International League Against Epilepsy 1 + 2 status was considered as the primary measure of seizure freedom. Complications in this study are defined as unwanted, unexpected, and uncommon events following a surgical procedure. 23 In our study, we largely adhered to the classification system set forth by Rydenhag and Silander, 23 with one modification: we extended the time frame for defining both 'major' and 'minor' complications from 3 months to 6 months following a more recent proposal by Bjellvi et al. 24 Accordingly, 'major' complications are those that persist for more than 6 months or are defined as persistent in the original studies, while 'minor' complications are those that are resolved within this 6-month period or are defined as transient in the original studies. Minor VFDs, affective disorders, or memory deficits were not considered as complications, as they were categorized as expected adverse effects rather than complications. 23 The primary endpoints also included the incidence of visual field deficits identified through perimetry. VFDs were those explicitly identified and labeled as such in the original studies; no additional criteria for the size or degree were imposed. Additionally, cognitive outcomes were assessed using specific neuropsychological tests: contextual verbal memory assessed by WMS-III LM-I or LM-II, 19 verbal learning and memory assessed by California verbal learning test/Reyauditory verbal learning test, 20,21 and confrontational naming assessed by Boston Naming Test. 22

The secondary endpoints of this study aimed to address 2 aspects concerning mesial temporal lobe sclerosis (MTLS). First, we assessed the in fluence of the MTLS-ratio on the overall seizure freedom rates. Second, we evaluated the comparative ef ficacy of the interventions specifically in achieving seizure freedom among patients diagnosed with MTLS.

## Statistical Analysis

In this meta-analysis, data from studies comparing therapeutic outcomes between laser interstitial thermal therapy (MRgLITT) and TLR in patients with DRE were synthesized. We performed all statistical analyses using R (version 4.3.1) 25 together with the meta package (6.5 e 0), 26 the metafor package (4.2 e 0), 27 and the forestplot package (3.1.1). 28 The primary outcomes of interest included seizure freedom, visual field de ficits, minor and major complications, and cognitive function. Proportions for each study were obtained, and effect sizes were calculated using the inverse variance method. Separate meta-analyses for the MRgLITT and TLR groups were conducted for each outcome, with random-effects models employed to account for anticipated between-study heterogeneity. A Z-test was conducted to analyze the statistical differences between the outcomes of MRgLITT and TLR, with a P value of less than 0.05 considered signi ficant.

The degree of heterogeneity among studies was assessed through the calculation of tau ( s ) and I 2 statistics, with values over 50% indicating substantial heterogeneity. A test of heterogeneity was also performed to evaluate whether the observed variability exceeded that expected by chance. Baujat plots were used to visually identify studies contributing most to heterogeneity. In fluence analysis was performed to examine the impact of individual studies on the overall results. This process identi fies studies that, if excluded, would lead to a signi ficant change in the overall effect size or heterogeneity, suggesting they have a disproportionate in fluence on the metaanalysis outcome.

Potential publication bias was evaluated using 2 methods: visual inspection of funnel plots and Egger ’ s regression test. These methods help identify any asymmetry that could suggest a bias toward publishing studies with more positive results. To compare the overall outcomes between MRgLITT and TLR, the seizure freedom proportion from both metaanalyses were compared using the Z-test. All reported P values are two-tailed, with values less than 0.05 considered statistically signi ficant. ## Subgroup Analysis

A subgroup analysis was conducted to analyze the potential relationship between the ratio of mesial temporal sclerosis (MTLS) and seizure-free outcomes, speci fically investigating whether a higher MTLS ratio in fluences the seizure freedom rate and exploring the possible interaction between the MTLS ratio and the effectiveness of each intervention (MRgLITT and TLR) in achieving seizure freedom.

Upon observing high heterogeneity in the TLR group, a post-hoc analysis was conducted to determine if MTLS is associated speci fically with the observed heterogeneity within the TLR group. The analysis was carried out using a mixedeffects meta-regression model, incorporating both MTLS ratio and intervention type as moderators. For this analysis, the MTLS ratio was calculated based on the overall cohort size, with an assumption that patients with more than 24 months of follow-up have the same rate of MTLS as those without such follow-up. This assumption, made due to the lack of individual patient data, was deemed necessary for the accuracy and integrity of the subgroup analysis.

## Quality Assessment

To assess the quality of the included studies, the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale was used. 29 This scale evaluates each study in 3 domains: selection of participants, comparability of study groups, and the ascertainment of outcomes. A star system is used to allow for a semiquantitative assessment, with more stars indicating higher quality.

## Grading and Recommendations

The quality of evidence and the strength of recommendations were assessed using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations (GRADE) approach. 30 This method involves an assessment of the quality of evidence across studies for each important outcome, considering study limitations, inconsistency of results, indirectness of evidence, imprecision, and reporting bias.

## Neuropsychological Outcomes

Assessing cognitive outcomes postsurgery presents challenges due to variations in neuropsychological methodologies and types of data reported, necessitating a descriptive analysis.

Studies showed mixed results for hippocampal-dependent functions postMRgLITT. Significant declines in verbal learning and delayed verbal memory, especially postablation of the dominant temporal lobe, were reported at the group level, although contextual memory was better preserved. 52,71 A significant decline in short delayed recall was observed, particularly in patients with MTLS and larger ablated volumes in the dominant temporal lobe. 72 While no significant group-level changes were reported in some studies, individual declines were observed, with some improvements noted after ablation in the non-dominant hemisphere. 68,73 Two studies did not report any data on statistical change at all. 51,74 Four out of six studies observed declines in verbal learning/memory post-MRgLITT; no decline in confrontation naming was seen.

Cognitive outcomes following TLR showed that dominant (most often left) TLR often led to a decline in verbal memory, 31 , 46 , 75-79 whereas nondominant (most often right) TLR could result in signi ficant improvement in these functions. 76 , 78 Two studies observed no signi ficant changes in verbal learning or memory following TLR. 80 , 81 Regarding confrontation naming, a decline was reported in certain TLR studies, 34 , 82 while no signi ficant changes were observed in others. 56 , 83 Overall, 7 of 9 studies reported declines in verbal learning/memory after resection in the dominant hemisphere, and 2 out of 4 studies showed a signi ficant decline in confrontational naming.

## MTLS Subgroup Analyses

A meta-analysis was conducted to explore the relationship between the ratio of mesial temporal sclerosis (MTLS) and seizure freedom, to evaluate the comparative effectiveness of TLR and MRgLITT in patients with MTLS, as well as to determine if MTLS is associated speci fically with the observed heterogeneity within the TLR groups seizure outcome. A total of 24 studies were included in the analysis, 18 TLR 31-39 , 41-49 and 6 MRgLITT. 50 , 52-55

The results did not reveal a signi ficant association between the MTLS ratio and seizure freedom ( P = 0.31), indicating that the proportion of patients with MTLS did not have a signi ficant impact on the seizure freedom rate. In terms of intervention type, there was no signi ficant difference in seizure outcome between TLR and MRgLITT for the MTLS group ( P = 0.63).

## DISCUSSION

In this comprehensive review and metaanalysis, we sought to compare the ef ficacy and safety of TLR, including ATL, sAHE, and MRgLITT for the treatment of temporal lobe epilepsy. TLR emerges as superior in achieving seizure freedom beyond 24 months, a finding that aligns with a previously published meta-analysis, which had a shorter follow-up. 12 Concerning VFD, the 2 methods appear to be comparable, although there was a nonsigni ficant trend in favor of MRgLITT. Although only reported in 2 studies, the overall incidence of VFDs after MRgLITT was higher than expected. There are multiple potential causes of VFD from MRgLITT including injury to the optic radiation from overablation or direct injury during catheter placement. 84 , 85 VFDs could also occur due to injury to the optic tract or the lateral geniculate nucleus due to catheter misplacement, thermal injury, or cytotoxic edema. 10 , 86 When examining the overall complication rates and major complication rate, no signi ficant difference was observed between the 2 interventions, even though TLR had numerically more overall complications, a conclusion that is consistent with an existing metaanalysis. 12 However, TLR presents a statistically greater risk for transient complications. Regarding cognitive outcomes, MRgLITT seems to have a more favorable outcome than TLR regarding confrontation naming, while studies are still too heterogenous to allow firm conclusions on verbal learning and memory. This is an interesting and important field of research that surely will present more data in the coming years.

While the primary focus of our analysis was on the duration of complications, the type and severity of these complications between TLR and MRgLITT deserve mention. Specifically, the majority of major complications for TLR are hemiparesis and hydrocephalus requiring shunting, accounting for 77% of the major complications. These arise largely due to the anatomical structures at risk during TLR, such as the anterior choroidal artery, which when injured can result in hemiparesis. Additionally, access through the temporal horn can cause intraventricular bleeding and hydrocephalus. In contrast, MRgLITT complications often result from thermal damage to anatomical structures of the oculomotor nerve, trochlear nerve, or optic tract, leading to double vision or hemianopia. The most common major complication for MRgLITT is diplopia, comprising 38% of the major complications. Although we lack data on how these complications impact patients' lives, one could argue that the complications associated with TLR are potentially more severe, given the critical anatomical structures at risk. Regarding minor complications, TLR more commonly results in hemorrhage/hematoma, aphasia/ dysphasia, and hemiparesis, representing 56% of all minor complications. For MRgLITT, the landscape of minor complications is more varied, but the most common are homonymous hemianopia and diplopia, accounting for 42%.

Our decision to differentiate between expected adverse events and unexpected complications is based on our aim to distinctly assess the unexpected outcomes of the surgical interventions. This does not imply that the potential occurrence of expected adverse events (which all patients receive information about in the presurgical counseling) is unimportant in the overall assessment of patient outcomes.

Our results on the cognitive outcomes after TLR and MRgLITT are consistent with the findings of Kohlhase et al. 12 In our analysis, 4 out of 6 studies on MRgLITT reported signi ficant declines at group level in verbal learning and/or delayed recall, with no studies noting a decline in confrontation naming ability. This is consistent with Kohlhase et al. ’ s observation of cognitive decline, particularly in verbal learning and memory, after treatment in the dominant temporal lobe. For TLR, our findings show that 7 out of 9 studies observed a signi ficant decline in verbal learning after resection in the dominant hemisphere, and 2 out of 4 studies reported a signi ficant decline in confrontation naming, making MRgLITT favorable in this regard. Standardized studies with comparative groups are needed to allow for a more robust statistical analysis and to draw firm conclusions on this important topic. One such study has been performed, which showed that verbal memory function was better

We decided to combine ATL and sAHE in order to increase the statistical power and external validity of our analysis. We acknowledge that there may be differences in outcomes between speci fic procedures that are not captured by the present analysis. In previous meta-analyses, Hu et al. 88 reported ATL to be superior to sAHE in terms of odds of seizure freedom; however, Kuang et al., Xu et al., and Kohlhase et al. found no signi ficant difference in the odds of seizure freedom between ATL and sAHE. 12 , 89 , 90

## MTLS Subgroup Analysis

The subgroup analysis revealed no significant difference in overall seizure freedom or intervention effectiveness for patients with mesial temporal lobe sclerosis (MTLS). However, it is worth noting that the assumption that patients with more than 24 months of follow-up have the same MTLS ratio as those without such long-term follow-up could introduce bias into these findings. This assumption was made due to data constraints and should be considered when interpreting the results. Future studies with individual patient data could offer a more nuanced understanding of the relationship between MTLS and seizure outcomes.

## Implications for Clinical Practice

The findings of this meta-analysis offer important insights for both surgeons and patients. As TLR is more effective at providing long-term seizure freedom than MRgLITT, TLR should be the preferred option when achieving long-term seizure freedom is the priority. However, if preserving visual function is of particular concern, MRgLITT might be the preferred intervention due to its trend toward fewer VFDs, though this finding needs to be validated in future studies. The risk profiles are also crucial in clinical decisionmaking; while TLR is associated with a higher rate of transient or minor complications, it does not result in a higher rate of major complications compared to MRgLITT. This suggests that both options carry similar risks of serious complications, but complications following MRgLITT may allow for faster recovery.

<!-- page: 9 -->

Regarding cognitive outcomes, there are indications that MRgLITT might better preserve verbal memory function than TLR.

It is essential to interpret the ef ficacy of MRgLITT and TLR within the context of each patient ’ s unique medical situation and in collaboration with the treating medical team. The effectiveness of MRgLITT is particularly dependent on the epileptogenic zone being localized strictly to the medial temporal lobe. In cases where the zone extends beyond this region, TLR may offer a more viable treatment option. Additionally, patient-speci fic factors such as age and overall health play a signi ficant role in treatment selection. Younger patients might be better candidates for open surgical procedures due to a lower risk of complications and better tolerance. Conversely, elderly patients might bene fit more from MRgLITT, given its favorable risk pro file for minor complications. 23 , 91

## Stability and Reliability of the Results

The meta-analysis revealed varying levels of heterogeneity across different outcomes and intervention types. Substantial heterogeneity was observed in the TLR group across several outcomes. One contributing factor to this high heterogeneity could be the combination of different resection methods under the umbrella term “ TLR, ” though high heterogeneity has also been reported in other meta-analyses when analyzing ATL and sAHE separate. 12 Efforts to explain the TLR group ’ s high heterogeneity in seizure outcomes through a subgroup analysis based on MTLS ratio were unsuccessful, leaving the source of this variability unaccounted for. In contrast, the MRgLITT group generally exhibited low to moderate heterogeneity across these outcomes. The zero heterogeneity observed in MRgLITT major complication outcome might suggest result stability; it could also indicate that the existing dataset is not comprehensive enough to re flect underlying variability.

The observed heterogeneity in reporting complications, particularly within the TLR group, raises important considerations. This variability could be attributed to multiple factors, such as differing surgical techniques, variations in patient characteristics, or inconsistencies in complication reporting across studies since there is no agreed-on common classification of complications. Given their transient nature, minor complications might be more likely to be underreported or inconsistently captured, which could contribute to the observed high heterogeneity. As such, caution is advised when interpreting the complication rates, especially for minor complications, as they might not fully capture the true rates of complications.

Publication bias and study in fluence were assessed. The TLR group showed signi ficant publication bias for seizure outcomes and VFDs, warranting caution in interpretation as they might be overreporting on more favorable outcomes. The MRgLITT group displayed no such bias for seizure outcomes or complications, although limited data for VFDs precluded a thorough analysis. In fluence analysis suggested high stability and generalizability for both TLR and MRgLITT, suggesting that the results are not overly sensitive to any single study.

In summary, the meta-analysis exhibits varying degrees of result stability, with the TLR group generally showing higher heterogeneity and potential for publication bias. Despite these concerns, in fluence analyses for both groups generally suggest that the findings are stable and robust, although the TLR group ’ s high heterogeneity for some outcomes remains unexplained. Further research, in particular comparative studies of TLR and MRgLITT assessing VFD with formal perimetry, is needed to strengthen the meta-analysis ’ s robustness.

A recent study with a large cohort has also been published after the completion of the literature search, which reports on seizure outcomes and complications after MRgLITT. 92 Importantly, the findings of this new study align closely with our own results. Given the similarity in outcomes and the already high stability of our meta-analysis, the exclusion of this new study is unlikely to signi ficantly affect our conclusions.

## Limitations

A significant limitation of this metaanalysis lies in the sample size for visual field deficits (VFDs) within the MRgLITT studies. The limited number of patients who underwent perimetry testing restricts the statistical power of the results and the conclusions that can be drawn. Larger cohorts, including formal perimetry with grading of VFDs and patient-relevant outcomes such as subjective symptoms and impact on driving, are needed. The overall high degree of heterogeneity in the TLR group is another limitation. Although the study attempted to explain this via subgroup analyses, the results remain inconclusive. This heterogeneity can affect the generalizability of the findings and calls for more standardized reporting in future studies. Additionally, our analysis of cognitive outcomes is also limited to a descriptive comparison due to challenges in data reporting as well as the variability in metrics used to define significance across studies. Potential bias in patient selection is also a concern, as different interventions may have been chosen based on individual patient characteristics. Furthermore, by including only patients with longer follow-up periods, there is a potential bias toward better outcomes, as patients with worse outcomes might be more likely to be lost to follow-up. However, loss to follow-upwasminimal in most studies, as indicated by the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale assessments.

## Future Directions in the Surgical Treatment of Temporal Lobe Epilepsy

The surgical management of temporal lobe epilepsy has seen signi ficant advancements in operative techniques, with more minimally invasive, precise microneurosurgery. Laser ablation has become a viable surgical option for TLE patients, even though rates of seizure freedom are lower. Future directions should focus on improving existing techniques and providing homogeneous data on visual field examinations and cognitive testing for laser ablation. With more surgical options, treatment can be offered to patients who previously were reluctant to undergo epilepsy surgery for fear of having open brain surgery.

In-Depth Analysis of VFD Rates: Continued research into VFDs following MRgLITT using perimetry would help clarify the differences in VFD rate between TLR and MRgLITT.

Standardized Cognitive Testing Protocols: Developing standardized studies with comparative groups for cognitive testing related to temporal lobe functions is essential. This would lead to more homogeneous data, facilitating a more accurate and detailed comparison of cognitive outcomes between TLR and MRgLITT.

<!-- page: 10 -->

Optimizing Minimally Invasive Techniques: Further research is needed to optimize MRgLITT and other minimally invasive procedures, such as radiofrequency thermocoagulation. As these techniques are relatively novel, ongoing research should focus on re fining surgical techniques to enhance ef ficacy and safety.

## CONCLUSIONS

Both TLR and MRgLITT have been proven to be effective and safe interventions for managing mTLE. However, our metaanalysis reveals speci fic advantages and considerations for each method. TLR is superior in achieving long-term seizure freedom, setting it apart as a more effective choice at achieving seizure control. On the other hand, MRgLITT and TLR are comparable in terms of overall and major complication rates, although TLR tends to result in more minor complications. MRgLITT also has a favorable outcome regarding confrontation naming, while verbal memory outcomes need to be more thoroughly investigated to be able to draw firm conclusions.
