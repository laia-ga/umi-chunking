# Single Administration of Bimatoprost Implant

## Effects on 24-Hour Intraocular Pressure and 1-Year Outcomes

Robert N. Weinreb, MD, 1 William C. Christie, MD, 2 Felipe A. Medeiros, MD, PhD, 3 E. Randy Craven, MD, 4 Kimmie Kim, PhD, 4 Ashley Nguyen, PharmD, 4 Marina Bejanian, PhD, 4 David L. Wirta, MD 5

Purpose: To evaluate the effects of a single bimatoprost implant administration on 24-hour intraocular pressure (IOP) lowering at 8 weeks, and 1-year IOP-lowering ef ficacy and safety outcomes.

Design: Multicenter, open-label, 12-month, phase 3b study (NCT04285580).

Participants: Adults with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension.

Methods: Participants (n = 31) received 10m g bimatoprost implant in the study eye on day 1; IOP (sitting and/or supine) was measured with pneumatonometry every 2 hours throughout a 24-hour period at baseline and week 8. IOP was measured by Goldmann applanation tonometry (GAT) at hour 0 (8 AM 1 hour) at baseline, weeks 8 and 16, and months 6, 9, and 12.

Main Outcome Measures: The primary endpoint was the week-8 hour-matched change from baseline in habitual position IOP over 24 hours assessed with pneumatonometry. Hour 0 IOP change from baseline measured with GAT in study eyes that received no additional (rescue) IOP-lowering treatment, treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), and central corneal endothelial cell density (CECD) were evaluated through 12 months.

Results: The mean (standard deviation [SD]) baseline IOP at hour 0 was 24.2 (2.70) mmHg and 25.3 (7.15)

mmHg by GAT and pneumatonometry, respectively. Pneumatonometer measurements of IOP taken over 24 hours at week 8 with the participant in habitual position (sitting from 8 AM to 10 PM , supine from 12 AM to 6 AM ) showed

consistent IOP lowering through the day and night and reduced fluctuation in IOP. The range in IOP measurements over 24 hours was reduced from baseline by a mean (SD) of 1.6 (2.98) mmHg. All 31 bimatoprost implant e treated

participants completed the 12-month study; 23 (74%) required no rescue IOP-lowering treatment. The mean (SD)

IOP reduction from baseline at month 12 in nonrescued eyes was 4.3 (3.35) mmHg. The most common TEAE was conjunctival hyperemia (incidence 35.5%, 11/31). No implant-treated eye had a 15% loss in CECD from baseline.

Conclusions: A single intracameral administration of the bimatoprost implant lowered IOP in the habitual position consistently throughout the day and night at week 8. The majority of participants continued to have reduced IOP for 1 year without additional therapy. The 1-year safety pro file was favorable.

Treatment in glaucoma aims to lower the intraocular pressure (IOP) to reduce the risk of glaucomatous visual field loss. However, IOP is affected by factors including body position and circadian rhythm. 1 , 2 Diurnal and nocturnal fluctuations in IOP occur in patients with elevated IOP as well as normal controls. 3 e 7 Intraocular pressure lowering through the day and night is important for patients with glaucoma, because peaks in IOP often occur outside of office hours and are likely to contribute to glaucoma progression. 8 , 9

Although some classes of topical IOP-lowering medications including beta-blockers demonstrate reduced ef ficacy during the night, 9 e 11 topical prostaglandin analogs/prostamides (PGAs) including bimatoprost have been shown to effectively lower IOP over 24 hours. 10 e 15 Bimatoprost implant 10 m g (Durysta; Allergan, an AbbVie company) is a biodegradable implant used to lower IOP in patients with open-angle glaucoma (OAG) or ocular hypertension (OHT). Two identically designed phase 3 studies demonstrated noninferiority of a single administration of either a 10or 15m g bimatoprost implant to twice-daily topical timolol maleate 0.5% in IOP lowering through 12 weeks. 16 , 18 Intraocular pressure lowering was assessed in these phase 3 studies using measurements taken at hour 0 (8 AM 1 hour) and hour 2 with Goldmann applanation tonometry (GAT) at baseline and weeks 2, 6, and 12. 16 , 18 In an earlier phase 1/2 study using the bimatoprost implant in dose strengths of 6, 10, 15, and 20 m g, IOP changes from baseline at diurnal measurements from 10 AM to 4 PM were consistent with the IOP change from baseline at the primary timepoint of 8 AM . 19

To our knowledge, the effects of the bimatoprost implant on IOP outside of normal of fice hours have not been reported. A single intracameral administration per eye of a 10 m g bimatoprost implant is currently approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for lowering IOP in OAG and OHT. The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the effects of a single 10m g bimatoprost implant administration on 24-hour IOP lowering assessed with pneumatonometry at 8 weeks after administration in patients with OAG and OHT. Pneumatonometry was used to allow IOP to be measured in the supine position. Safety and IOP lowering at hour 0 (8 AM ) by GAT were assessed through 12 months.

### Methods

This multicenter (5 sites), open-label, 12-month, phase 3b study evaluated the 24-hour IOP-lowering effect of the bimatoprost implant in patients with OAG or OHT. The study was conducted from June 11, 2020, to May 14, 2022, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Health Information Portability and Accountability Act. Institutional review board or ethics committee approval was obtained at each site, and all participants provided written informed consent before screening. The study is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov with the identi fier NCT04285580.

#### Participants

Study participants were nonsmoking adults with a diagnosis of OAG or OHT requiring IOP-lowering treatment in at least 1 eye (the study eye). The study eye was required to have an open iridocorneal angle by clinical gonioscopy and baseline IOP after washout of 22 to 34 mmHg by GAT. The key exclusion criteria included previous administration of a bimatoprost implant in the study eye, history of laser trabeculoplasty in the study eye, history or evidence of clinically relevant substantial ocular trauma in the study eye, and history or evidence of complicated cataract surgery or phakic intraocular lens insertion for refractive error correction in the study eye.

##### Study Design

Figure 1 shows a schematic of the study design. After a screening visit, patients potentially eligible for the study discontinued use of IOP-lowering medications in both eyes. Minimum washout times were 4 days for parasympathomimetics and carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, 14 days for sympathomimetics and alpha agonists, and 28 days for b -blockers, r kinase inhibitors, and PGAs. Eligible patients were enrolled in the study following baseline evaluations at an of fice visit and underwent a 24-hour IOP assessment at a sleep lab visit within the next 7 days. After the baseline sleep lab visit, participants were administered a 10m g bimatoprost implant in the study eye on day 1. The implant was administered intracamerally using sterile technique and a pre filled, single-use, 28gauge applicator under standard aseptic conditions for intraocular procedures. 16 Safety was assessed at an of fice visit on day 2 and by telephone on day 8. An of fice visit at week 8 was followed by a sleep lab visit within 7 days at week 8, and follow-up of fice visits were scheduled at week 16 and months 6, 9, and 12 (study exit). At 1 site, an additional group of participants (a reference cohort)

was enrolled and assigned by the investigator to receive topical bimatoprost 0.01% in the study eye once daily at 8 PM 1 hour, starting on day 1. The participants in the reference cohort were exited from the study after completing the week 8 sleep lab visit. Throughout the study, fellow eyes of all participants were administered standard-of-care treatment, provided that in the opinion of the investigator, there was no known crossover effect to the study eye.

At sleep lab visits, IOP was measured over 24 hours with a Reichert Model 30 pneumatonometer (Reichert, Inc) by a site staff member masked to the study treatment and study eye. Measurements were taken every 2 hours at the following time points ( 30 minutes): 8 AM , 10 AM , 12 PM , 2 PM , 4 PM , 6 PM , 8 PM , 10 PM , 12 AM , 2 AM , 4 AM , and 6 AM . Diurnal IOP measurements, de fined as the IOP measurements between 8 AM ( 30 minutes) and 10 PM ( 30 minutes), were taken first with the participant supine after 5 minutes at rest, then sitting (habitual position) after 5 minutes at rest. Lights in individual sleep rooms were turned off at 11 PM , and nocturnal IOP measurements, de fined as the IOP measurements between 12 AM ( 30 minutes) and 6 AM ( 30 minutes), were taken under dim lighting with participants in habitual (supine) position, after 5 minutes rest in the same position. At of fice visits, IOP was measured at hour 0 (8 1 hour) by

AM GAT with the participant seated at the tonometer. These IOP measurements were taken using a 2-person reading method. 20 In assessments of IOP at both the sleep lab and of fice visits, IOP in the right eye was measured first, then the left eye. Two IOP measurements were taken for each eye, and if the measurements differed by > 1 mmHg, a third measurement was taken. The mean of 2 measurements, or the median of 3 measurements, was used for analysis.

Safety evaluations conducted at the of fice visits included treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), best-corrected visual acuity, slit lamp biomicroscopy, dilated ophthalmoscopy, visual fields, pachymetry, and gonioscopy with bimatoprost implant assessment. Corneal endothelial cell density (CECD) was assessed by the investigator using specular microscopy performed on the central cornea and automated analysis. Rescue IOP-lowering treatment could be initiated in the study eye if deemed necessary for the safety of the participant because of inadequate control of IOP, as determined by the investigator.

##### Outcome Measures

The primary ef ficacy endpoint was the hour-matched change from baseline in the study eye IOP at week 8, assessed using the pneumatonometer with the participant in habitual position (sitting from 8 AM to 10 PM , supine from 12 AM to 6 AM ). Change from baseline in the range of IOP in bimatoprost implant e treated eyes at the week 8 sleep lab visit was the secondary ef ficacy endpoint. Other preplanned ef ficacy endpoints included the hour-matched IOP change from baseline in the supine position at the week 8 sleep lab visit, the hour 0 IOP change from baseline through month 12 (measured with GAT at of fice visits), and the time to initial use of nonstudy (rescue) IOP-lowering treatment.

##### Data Analysis

Two database locks were planned for the study. The first database lock occurred when the last participant remaining in the study completed the week 8 sleep lab visit, and the final database lock occurred at study completion. The primary and secondary ef ficacy analyses used data from the first database lock; the other ef ficacy and safety analyses used data from the final database lock. Ef ficacy outcomes were evaluated using the full analysis set of all participants who received study intervention (bimatoprost implant or topical bimatoprost 0.01%) in the study eye and had at least 1 postbaseline IOP assessment in the study eye that was used for ef ficacy analyses. To avoid confounding, IOP measurements obtained after initiating the use of rescue IOP-lowering treatment in an eye were excluded from analysis. Safety outcomes were evaluated in the safety population of all participants who received a study intervention.

In the primary ef ficacy analysis, diurnal and nocturnal IOP in the habitual position and the corresponding hour-matched change from baseline in habitual position IOP were evaluated by visit and by timepoint. The mean hour-matched, habitual position IOP change from baseline at 12 timepoints across the 24-hour period with 95% con fidence intervals (CIs) based on paired t tests were calculated for study eyes in the bimatoprost implant group. The

Time to initial use of rescue IOP-lowering treatment (medication and/or procedure) from the date of bimatoprost implant administration was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method.

##### Sample Size Determination

The sample size was based on practical considerations. Approximately 35 participants were planned to be enrolled (25 to 30 in the bimatoprost implant group and 5 in the reference cohort). A sample size of 25 to 30 participants in the bimatoprost implant group was planned to achieve a minimum of 20 participants with suf ficient data at baseline and week 8 to evaluate the 24-hour IOP-lowering effect of the bimatoprost implant, assuming a premature discontinuation rate of up to 33%. The study was not powered for statistical comparison between the bimatoprost implant group and the reference cohort.

#### Results

A total of 31 patients were enrolled in the bimatoprost implant group; all 31 participants received a 10 m g bimatoprost implant in the study eye and completed the 12-month study. The mean study duration for study participants in the bimatoprost implant group was 361.7 days (range, 351 e 379 days). Six patients at one site were enrolled and assigned to the reference group. All 6 participants in the reference group received once-daily topical bimatoprost 0.01% in the study eye. Five (83.3%) of these participants completed the week 8 study visit and were exited from the study, and 1 (16.7%) discontinued the study early because of use of an excluded medication. The mean duration of the study for the reference group participants was 57.5 days (range, 56 e 60 days).

Baseline demographics and ophthalmic characteristics were similar in the bimatoprost implant group and the total study population ( Table 2 ). The mean age of participants in the bimatoprost implant group was 70.5 years (range, 40 e 90 years), 58.1% (18/31) were female, 71.0% (22/31) were White, and 80.6% (25/31) had a diagnosis of primary OAG in the study eye. Age and race were similar in the reference group, but the majority of participants (66.7%, 4/6) in the reference group were male and 50% (3/6) had a diagnosis of OHT in the study eye ( Table 2 ).

Sleep lab IOP measurements showed that habitual position IOP measured with a pneumatonometer was consistently reduced throughout the day and night at 8 weeks after bimatoprost implant administration ( Fig 2 ). The mean hour-matched, habitual position, IOP change from baseline over 24 hours by pneumatonometry ranged from 3.7 to e 1.7 mmHg in bimatoprost implant e treated study eyes (n = 31); the range was e 3.7 to e 1.8 mmHg across diurnal timepoints (measurements taken with the participant sitting) and e 2.4 to e 1.7 mmHg across nocturnal timepoints (measurements taken with the participant in the supine position). In the reference group, study eyes treated with once-daily bimatoprost 0.01% demonstrated consistent IOP reduction throughout the day at week 8, with the mean hour-matched, habitual position IOP change from baseline over 24 hours by pneumatonometry ranging from e 5.0 to e 1.8 mmHg (n = 5).

Fluctuation in habitual position IOP over 24 hours was reduced after bimatoprost implant administration. The mean range in IOP over 24 hours, measured in habitual position by pneumatonometry at sleep lab visits, was 9.9 mmHg at baseline and 8.3 mmHg at week 8, and the mean change from baseline in IOP range at week 8 was 1.6 mmHg ( Table 3 ). This reduction in 24-hour habitual position IOP fluctuation occurred primarily because of a reduction in diurnal IOP fluctuation (i.e., a reduction in the range of IOP values obtained by pneumatonometry in measurements taken in a sitting position from 8 AM to 10 PM ) ( Table 3 ). In the reference group treated with bimatoprost 0.01%, the mean (standard deviation [SD]) change from baseline in range of habitual position over 24 hours at the week 8 sleep lab visit was + 0.7 (3.90) mmHg (median: e 0.5 mmHg; n = 5).

Measurements of IOP taken at of fice visits with a Goldmann applanation tonometer differed somewhat from those taken at the sleep lab visits with a pneumatonometer ( Table 4 ). Goldmann applanation IOP measurements at 8 AM at the week 8 of fice visit showed a mean (SD) change from baseline IOP of e 6.8 (3.1) mmHg in bimatoprost implant e treated eyes, whereas pneumatonometry (sitting) measurements at 8 AM at the week 8 sleep lab visit showed a mean (SD) change from baseline IOP of e 3.7 (5.7) mmHg in bimatoprost implant e treated eyes ( Table 4 ). Intraocular pressure lowering at each diurnal timepoint was demonstrated by pneumatonometry at week 8 in bimatoprost implant e treated eyes when measurements were taken in the supine as well as the sitting (habitual) position ( Table S5 , available at www.ophthalmolo gyglaucoma.org ).

At subsequent of fice visits, most study eyes in the bimatoprost implant group demonstrated sustained IOP lowering without use of rescue IOP-lowering treatment through month 12 (study exit) after bimatoprost implant administration ( Fig 3 ). The percentage of participants in the bimatoprost implant group requiring no nonstudy (rescue) IOP-lowering treatment in the study eye was 100% (31/31) at week 8 and remained high (74.2%, 23/31) at month 12. #### Discussion

This study demonstrated that the bimatoprost implant provides 24-hour IOP lowering in patients with OAG and OHT. Measurements of IOP in the habitual position showed that the bimatoprost implant lowered IOP from baseline consistently through both the day and night at 8 weeks after administration. Previous studies showed that topical treatment with oncedaily bimatoprost 0.01% reduces habitual position IOP through the day and night. 11 , 15 However, in those studies, mean IOP reductions during the diurnal period were approximately twice as large as those during the nocturnal period. 11 , 15 In the present study, the mean habitual position IOP reduction was 2.6 mmHg during the diurnal period and 2.1 mmHg during the nocturnal period. These results show that the bimatoprost implant provided a more sustained IOP-lowering effect over 24 hours than oncedaily bimatoprost 0.01%.

Long-term IOP fluctuation has been demonstrated to be a risk factor for glaucomatous progression, 21 and it is thought that the amplitude of short-term (24-hour) IOP fluctuation also may be associated with the risk of progression. In this study, administration of the bimatoprost implant reduced 24-hour IOP fluctuation in study eyes at week 8. No reduction in 24-hour IOP fluctuation was observed in study eyes in the reference cohort, but this may have been because of the small sample size, as results from previous studies have suggested that topical treatment with PGAs including bimatoprost reduces 24-hour IOP fluctuation. 22

The IOP measurements at the sleep lab and of fice visits differed, but both pneumatonometry (at the sleep lab visits) and GAT (at the of fice visits) demonstrated IOP lowering at 8 AM , week 8 in bimatoprost implant e treated study eyes. The magnitude of IOP reduction measured with GAT (approximately 7 mmHg) was consistent with results of the ARTEMIS phase 3 bimatoprost implant studies. 16 , 18 The reasons for the discrepancy in observed IOP between the sleep lab and of fice visits are unknown. However, the IOP measurements at the of fice and sleep lab visits were taken using different methods of tonometry. Consistent with the present results, pneumatonometry has been reported to overestimate IOP relative to GAT. 23 Although both GAT and pneumatonometry readings are affected by central corneal thickness, measurements of IOP by pneumatonometry are also affected by the IOP itself. 24 The IOP measurements at the sleep lab and of fice visits were also taken by different assessors, and on different days.

The study design included longer follow-up after single implant administration than in the phase 3 ARTEMIS studies. The results showed that IOP lowering was sustained in the majority of patients for up to 1 year after a single implant administration. At 1 year, 74% (23/31) of the participants in the bimatoprost implant group had not required any additional IOP-lowering treatment in the study eye. The sustained IOP lowering was consistent with results of the phase 1/2 bimatoprost implant study, in which 28% (21/75) of study participants who received a 6-, 10-, 15-, or 20-mg dose strength of bimatoprost implant completed the 24-month study without requiring rescue topical IOP-lowering medication or a second administration of implant in the study eye. 25 We have proposed that sustained IOP lowering is observed in many patients after bimatoprost implant treatment, even after the implant has been fully depleted of bimatoprost and intraocular drug levels are negligible, because the high bimatoprost levels achieved in target tissues by the implant cause enhanced activation of matrix metalloproteinases, leading to durable remodeling of aqueous outflow pathways and sustained IOP lowering. 16,25e27

The bimatoprost implant was well tolerated in this study, and there were no TEAEs requiring implant removal or leading to study discontinuation. The most frequent TEAEs were ocular and mild or moderate in severity, and there were no serious treatment-related TEAEs. Importantly, no implant-treated eye demonstrated a 15% or greater loss in CECD from baseline at 1 year after the implant administration. A loss of CECD of 10% or less may not be reliable (because of errors in measurement) or clinically meaningful,28 but larger losses in CECD can be associated with decreases in corneal transparency, corneal edema, and reduced visual acuity.29 In the phase 3 ARTEMIS studies, where participants received 3 administrations of bimatoprost implant with a fixed 16-week dosing interval, CECD loss of 20% or greater occurred within 1 year of beginning treatment in approximately 5% of participants treated with the 10-mg bimatoprost implant.16,18 In the ARTEMIS studies, there were no TEAE reports of corneal endothelial cell loss before the second administration of the 10-mg bimatoprost implant.16,18 Similarly, in the present study, no clinically significant CECD occurred after a single administration of the bimatoprost implant.

A strength of this study was that, for each participant, all sleep lab IOP measurements were taken with the same pneumatonometer by an assessor masked to the study eye. This allowed the IOP to be measured in habitual position and prevented any potential differences between the diurnal and nocturnal IOP measurements caused by the use of different methods of tonometry for IOP measurements in an individual patient.

The small sample size was a study limitation. In addition, the effects of the bimatoprost implant on nocturnal IOP during undisturbed sleep were not evaluated. A reference cohort was included to allow con firmation that measurements of IOP lowering with a topical PGA were in line with those previously reported in the literature, but the study design did not support direct comparison of results

In summary, in this study the bimatoprost implant reduced IOP consistently through both the day and the night and reduced IOP fluctuation over 24 hours in patients with OAG and OHT. The bimatoprost implant demonstrated a favorable safety pro file in assessments through 1 year after a single administration. Furthermore, the majority of patients had sustained IOP lowering and required no additional IOPlowering treatment in the study eye throughout the 12month study. Patients treated with the bimatoprost implant have a high probability of needing no other IOP-lowering treatment for at least 1 year.

#### A Late-Onset Phacolytic Glaucoma

A 54-year-old man presented with sudden ocular pain, redness, and blurred vision, with elevated intraocular pressure in the left eye. He denied any diseases but experienced an ocular trauma 42 years ago. Slit-lamp examination revealed corneal edema, hyphema, and pupil dilation, with iridescent crystalline everywhere ( A ). Ultrasound biomicroscopy showed irregular lens morphology and angle recession ( B ). He was diagnosed with late-onset phacolytic glaucoma. Surgery revealed a plum-shaped amber lens nucleus with iridescent crystals everywhere ( C ). Pathological examination showed sparkling crystals associated with macrophages laden with soluble lens proteins, calcium oxalate, and cholesterol ( D ).
